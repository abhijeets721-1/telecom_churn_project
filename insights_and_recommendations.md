# Insights and Recommendations

## Headline
Out of **6,589** customers (the 454 newly joined customers are excluded because they have not had time to churn), **1,869 left, a churn rate of 28.37%**. That cost about **$3.68M in revenue**.

## What the data shows

| # | Insight | Evidence |
|---|---------|----------|
| 1 | One small group churns at almost 4x the rest | Month-to-Month + Fiber Optic + first 12 months: 753 customers, **81.67%** churn vs 21.49% for everyone else |
| 2 | Competitors are the main reason customers leave | 45% of all churned customers left for a competitor |
| 3 | Fiber Optic customers churn more | 42.1% churn rate |
| 4 | Offer E is the worst offer | 67.6% churn rate |
| 5 | Missing tech support goes with higher churn | 44.5% churn among internet customers without Premium Tech Support |
| 6 | Churn is concentrated in a few cities | San Diego: 66.55% churn, and it alone is 9.90% of all churn |
| 7 | Older customers leave more | Aged 60+: 37.90% vs 24-26% for every younger group |

## Recommendations

1. **Run a San Diego retention campaign first.** One city drives about 10% of all churn.
2. **Move new Month-to-Month Fiber Optic customers to 1-year plans** in their first year, when risk is highest (81.67%).
3. **Build a support and loyalty offer for customers aged 60+.**
4. **Bundle Premium Tech Support with plans and retire Offer E.**
5. **Track churn monthly by segment and by competitor reason** so the team can see what is working.

## Limits of this analysis
- Churn rate excludes the 454 "Joined" customers, so it describes customers who have had time to leave.
- The analysis shows association, not proof of cause. Offers and campaigns should be A/B tested before a full rollout.
- Churn rates for small groups can be noisy. The city chart only shows cities with 30+ customers.
